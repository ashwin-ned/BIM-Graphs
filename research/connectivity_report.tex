\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Exported IFC states geometry and semantics but not topology: across 25 real
Revit exports (257\,MB) there are \textbf{zero} \texttt{IfcRelSpaceBoundary}
instances, only 51\% of storeys contain any \texttt{IfcSpace}, and 12\% of
elements are assigned to a room. Recovering which rooms exist and which of them
actually connect is therefore the problem, and it cannot be scored on real
files because no real file states the answer. We build a procedurally generated
benchmark whose connectivity is known by construction and independently
verified by a second, unrelated mechanism (2{,}769/2{,}769 room pairs), and
report edge precision/recall against it as a function of how badly the IFC has
been damaged. Our method reaches \textbf{F1 0.996} on clean input and
\textbf{0.868} under damage, beating the strongest in-repo ablation by
\textbf{+0.085} (131 wins / 0 losses, paired Wilcoxon $p<10^{-6}$, $n=150$) and
an independently published external tool by \textbf{+0.495}. A graduated damage
sweep isolates room recovery as the sole binding constraint. We then report
what these numbers do \emph{not} establish: on the 25 real models we have only
necessary conditions, not accuracy, and we show why a partner company's
scene-graph delivery --- verified accurate to the integer --- still cannot
substitute for the annotation that would close that gap. This report
consolidates every connectivity result measured to date, with the command that
reproduces each.
\end{abstract}

\section{The benchmark and why it is synthetic}

No real IFC states room-to-room connectivity, so a real corpus offers nothing to
score against. \texttt{main/generate\_building.py} inverts the problem: the
answer is the \emph{input}. It lays out each floor by recursive rectangular
subdivision, then decides, for every geometrically adjacent room pair, whether
the pair is actually joined --- a door (\texttt{door\_ratio}, default 0.6), a
door-less opening (\texttt{open\_ratio}, default 0.15), or nothing at all. The
remainder are solid wall.

Those negatives are the single most important property of the corpus. If every
adjacent pair were connected, a trivial ``join every neighbour'' baseline would
score near-perfect and the benchmark could not distinguish any two methods. The
current corpus holds 2{,}049 rooms, 1{,}708 ground-truth edges (1{,}293 door,
265 open-passage, 150 vertical) and \textbf{1{,}211 adjacent-but-unconnected
negatives} across 150 two-storey buildings.

\paragraph{Ground truth is verified, not asserted.}
The generator and the recovery pipeline share an author, so a shared
misconception would produce a perfect score that means nothing.
\texttt{main/verify\_gt.py} therefore checks the ground truth by a different
mechanism: it rasterises the wall solids at 0.05\,m and flood-fills free space
at door height, asking whether one room's interior physically reaches
another's, rather than reasoning about polygon strips as the pipeline does.
Agreement is \textbf{2{,}769/2{,}769 room pairs (100.0\%)}.

Against \texttt{door\_near}, the strongest in-repo baseline, the margin is
\textbf{131 wins / 0 losses / 19 ties} on clean input (95\% CI
$[+0.074,+0.096]$) and 82/2/66 under L2 damage ($[+0.032,+0.050]$).

\paragraph{\texttt{schema} scores 0.000 at every level, and that is the point.}
The schema-trusting baseline is what most BIM-to-graph work does. It recovers
nothing because there is nothing in the file to read --- the same fact the
audit above reports, made operational.

\paragraph{The trivial baseline is not trivially beaten.} \texttt{adjacency}
achieves high recall by construction, so its precision is the number that
matters: 0.486 on clean input, joining through 1{,}211 solid walls. This is the
bar any real method must clear, and it is why the generator emits negatives.

\section{Damage sweep}

\texttt{main/damage\_ifc.py} applies graduated damage, each level reproducing a
pathology measured in the real corpus at its measured prevalence rather than an
invented one: L1 strips space boundaries (100\% of real models lack them --- and
a \emph{no-op} on generated files, which never had any); L2 deletes
\texttt{IfcSpace} on 49\% of storeys; L3 orphans the survivors from their
storey; L4 additionally drops all containment and rescales coordinates by 100
with the unit assignment detached.

L2 and L3 land identically across all 150 buildings (ours 0.8678). L4 differs on
exactly two buildings, \texttt{synth\_0012} ($0.880\!\to\!0.917$) and
\texttt{synth\_0135} ($0.963\!\to\!1.000$) --- both marginally \emph{higher},
mean 0.8683; the mechanism has not been investigated. The finding is that
hierarchy repair and empirical unit detection absorb L3 and L4 essentially
completely, so \textbf{only missing rooms degrade connectivity}. Room recovery,
not hierarchy or unit repair, is the binding constraint --- confirmed
independently by this sweep, by the baseline ablation, and by the distribution
of real-corpus failures.

\section{External baselines}

\paragraph{Ifc2Graph (scored).} Bj{\o}rnskov's published, open-source tool
voxelises \texttt{IfcSpace} meshes and reports two spaces adjacent if they share
a voxel boundary. Run out of the box (one one-line NumPy 2.x compatibility
patch) across all 300 buildings, zero crashes. It scores \textbf{below our own
naive \texttt{adjacency} ablation} on clean input: precision 0.335 at its
default 0.5\,m voxel pitch --- coarser than our 0.20\,m walls --- and it counts
rooms merely stacked with a floor slab between them as adjacent (verified
directly: it reports \texttt{S0R000 -> S1R000} with no stair near either room).
Its recall collapses $1.000\!\to\!0.436$ under L2 damage: having no
room-recovery stage, \texttt{IfcSpace} stripping makes true connections
literally invisible to it. Vertical-connection recall goes $150/150 \to 0/150$.

\paragraph{IFC2SB (built, run, not scorable).} RWTH-E3D's C++/OpenCASCADE tool
generates space boundaries from raw geometry. Building it required three fixes
to the vendor's own files, none algorithmic: a Dockerfile \texttt{FROM} line
with an inline comment that current BuildKit rejects; an \texttt{ADD} with no
following \texttt{WORKDIR}; and \texttt{src/Clip.cpp}'s dependency on CGAL,
absent from the README's prerequisites. It then crashes identically --- same
exception, same token position --- on both synthetic buildings tested,
producing no output on any of the 300 ground-truth-verified buildings. On real
files behaviour depends on how much \texttt{IfcSpace} the input already states:
with 53 stated rooms it produces real interior data (51 spaces, 3{,}058
boundaries, 81 candidate connections) before a late segfault; with 2 stated
rooms it completes cleanly but yields zero interior rooms.

There is no corpus on which both ``IFC2SB runs'' and ``we have verified ground
truth'' hold simultaneously, so no F1 exists to report. It is excluded from the
table above rather than misrepresented.

\section{Real corpus: necessary conditions, not accuracy}

The 25 real models yield \textbf{1{,}025 rooms} (681 stated, 344 recovered) and
161 vertical links; every graph loads, and navigation and QnA function on all
25. Validation is by \emph{necessary conditions} (\texttt{main/validate.py}) ---
conditions any correct building graph must satisfy:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llr}
\toprule
& \textbf{Condition} & \textbf{Pass} \\
\midrule
R2 & every door joins exactly two rooms      & \textbf{25/25} \\
R3 & every room has one storey parent        & \textbf{25/25} \\
R4 & areas and storey heights are physical   & \textbf{25/25} \\
R6 & elements lie inside their assigned room & \textbf{25/25} \\
\addlinespace
R1a & intra-storey connectivity $\geq 0.90$  & 9/25 \\
R1b & storeys mutually reachable             & 10/24 (1 n/a) \\
R5 & element--room coverage $\geq 0.50$      & 18/25 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{These cannot rank a graph, only falsify one.} Nothing here
establishes that a recovered corridor corresponds to a real corridor. The
temptation is to read intra-storey reachability as a quality score; it is not
one, and the corpus proves it --- the \texttt{adjacency} baseline, which joins
through 79 solid walls on known ground truth, scores \textbf{0.935} on that
metric against our 0.744. A metric that rewards over-connection ranks the worst
method first.

\paragraph{R1a failures are concentrated and explicable.} They fall
predominantly on open-plan, glazed buildings whose enclosure is curtain wall
rather than \texttt{IfcWall} --- one model holds 2{,}098 \texttt{IfcPlate}
against 51 \texttt{IfcWall} --- where there are few partitions to recover at
all. A separate probe (\texttt{main/proxy\_probe.py}) tested whether
misclassified \texttt{IfcBuildingElementProxy} elements were the cause: a
geometric signature recognising 98.1\% of known \texttt{IfcWall} found the proxy
population bimodal, and reclassifying all 259 wall-like proxies changed rooms,
doors and reachability by \textbf{exactly zero}. Ruled out, not assumed away.

\section{Goal resolution: the loop closed end to end}

Every result above scores one stage. \texttt{main/eval\_goal\_resolution.py}
asks the question the system exists to answer: given a functional query, is the
right room retrieved, and can a specific robot then reach it? Using the same
leave-one-out masking as the labelling study, resolution succeeds on
\textbf{308/593 = 0.519} of held-out real rooms, tracking per-class label
accuracy closely because it is the same prediction asked differently. For each
resolved goal, planning from the building entry succeeds for 0.169 of goals
under a wheeled profile and 0.461 under legged/human.

The honest caveat: any candidate of the right class counts as resolved. This
measures finding \emph{a} room of the right kind, not disambiguating ``the
kitchen on the 2nd floor'' from three others.

\section{A partner delivery, verified and declined}

Ramblr (partner company) ran their own IFC-to-scene-graph pipeline over 208 real
models --- of which our 25 are an exact byte-for-byte subset --- and proposed the
output as pseudo ground truth, to skip annotation.

We re-derived every number in their accuracy report from the raw files, using
\texttt{IfcRelVoidsElement}\,+\,\texttt{IfcRelFillsElement} chained through the
shared opening as an independent source of truth. Two counting bugs in our own
first attempts (element nodes repeated once per assigned room; each relation
stored on both endpoints) gave answers 6$\times$ and 2$\times$ off before being
found. After fixing them, \textbf{every aggregate matches exactly}: 3{,}510
relations checked, 3{,}103 confirmed, precision 0.884, 333 extra-wall and 13
true mistakes and 61 unjudgeable, 22{,}671 real pairs, recall 0.137, 179{,}020
element nodes with zero broken references, and the full per-class
element-coverage table down to the integer.

\textbf{The report is accurate; the data is still not usable as connectivity
ground truth}, for two independent reasons. It asserts the wrong relation ---
\emph{wall contains opening}, not \emph{which two rooms a door joins}. And it is
an open-world set: 86.3\% of real wall-opening relations are absent and 92/208
files hold none at all, so treating absence as evidence of non-existence would
penalise any method, ours included, for finding more correct connections than
the reference happens to contain. The same trap as reading reachability as
quality, in a different costume.

\paragraph{What verifying it did produce.} The wall-opening relations we
extracted to check Ramblr's work are themselves complete and native to the
files: 22{,}671 real pairs across 208 models, no vendor tool required. This
corpus also has substantial \texttt{IfcSpace} (188/208 files, median 71\% of
storeys) --- materially richer than the 25-model subset's headline numbers
suggest. Real, verifiable room-connectivity ground truth may therefore be
derivable for these 208 buildings without manual annotation, by combining the
native opening relations with room geometry and checking the result by the same
independent-mechanism discipline used for the synthetic corpus. That is the
largest open opportunity this project currently has.

\section{What is still unmeasured}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{No accuracy figure on real files}, for connectivity or for the
333 persisted room labels. Everything real is held-out or necessary-condition;
everything accurate is synthetic.
\item \textbf{Synthetic buildings are orthogonal and clean} by design, to
isolate connectivity recovery. Real files are harder, and the gap between
0.996 and what the method would score on annotated real data is unknown.
\item \textbf{Room recovery on open-plan floors} remains the binding
constraint, and proxies have been ruled out as the cause without a replacement
explanation being established.
\item \textbf{Four label classes never resolve} (outdoor, storage, retail,
service), and goal resolution inherits that failure exactly.
\end{enumerate}
